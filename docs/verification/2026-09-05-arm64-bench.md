## Command

```sh
CGO_ENABLED=0 GOARCH=arm64 go build -mod=vendor -o dist/neurosymbolic-engine ./cmd/neurosymbolic-engine
time dist/neurosymbolic-engine bench --json
```

`bench` was run with every flag at its default: 200 rules, 20 fields, 10,000
ticks, a 20ms period (50Hz), a 32MB RSS ceiling.

## Reading it

At a 20ms (20,000 µs) budget, the p99 tick cost of 616.5 µs leaves roughly
32x headroom, and even the single worst tick recorded (1307.9 µs) is more
than 15x under budget with zero overruns across all 10,000 ticks — this
engine's per-tick evaluate/arbitrate/compose/write path is not what limited
the donor's real robot. The donor's own measured ceiling was **~23 Hz**
end-to-end (`reachy-mini-cli/CLAUDE.md`) — a budget an order of magnitude
tighter than the 50 Hz (20ms) this library targets — and that ceiling came
from constructing SDK clients, contended sense processes and blocking
transport writes on the tick thread, none of which exist in this synthetic
load: it runs a null sink with a real 200-rule/20-field evaluator and nothing
else on the loop. That is exactly the comparison this record is for — the
rules/arbitration/composition core this repository is extracting is not the
bottleneck the donor hit; the seams `internal/compose` still has to wire to a
real transport, sense source and hardware SDK are. Steady-state RSS (10.3MB,
sampled at tick 1000 and again at tick 10,000, reporting the max of the two)
sits comfortably under the 32MB ceiling with no growth trend between the two
samples, which is what "steady-state" is meant to rule out: an evaluator that
leaks state per tick under a 200-rule load would have shown it by tick
10,000.
